# The top tier: what every screen is checked against first

Every screen, every time, is read first against Apple's eight design principles, then cross-checked against Nielsen's ten usability heuristics, before any claim in `design.md` (Timmy, 2026-11-05). The tier comes from authoritative, general sources, never from one screen's failures. Apple's principles come first because Apple's guidelines are the client's first reference; Nielsen's heuristics, drawn from a factor analysis of 249 usability problems, catch what Apple's words leave broad. Each item below is the source's own line, then the check a review answers in a sentence, yes or no with what on the screen shows it. The claims in `design.md` are filed under the Apple principle each serves, as its evidence, and name the heuristic each also answers.

Sources: Apple Human Interface Guidelines, Design principles, `reference/hig/design-principles.md`; Apple, Principles of great design, WWDC26 session 250, `reference/wwdc/wwdc2026-250.md`; Nielsen, 10 Usability Heuristics for User Interface Design, `reference/nngroup/ten-usability-heuristics.md`.

## Apple's eight, first

1. **Purpose: "Make something meaningful."** Create value; keep focused; find new ways to solve the problem. Check: what is this screen for, in one sentence in the person's words, and does every part on it serve that?
2. **Agency: "Let people do things their own way."** Stay out of the way; give people the freedom to explore; help people recover from mistakes. Check: can the person get straight to what they came for, leave any flow, and undo or recover from any slip without losing time or work?
3. **Responsibility: "Act in people's best interest."** Be fully transparent about what the product does and why; keep people's information safe. Check: does the screen say plainly what it will do and why it asks for anything, and does it collect only what it needs?
4. **Familiarity: "Build on what people know."** Use concepts that people know; keep visuals and interactions consistent; provide clear feedback. Check: does every control, word and gesture work as the platform and the rest of the app already taught, and does every action get a clear answer?
5. **Flexibility: "Adapt to diverse contexts and needs."** Design for everyone; preserve a person's context; consider a variety of input methods; approach every platform with intention. Check: does the screen work at the largest text size, with VoiceOver, without colour or motion, and with every input the device offers?
6. **Simplicity: "Be clear and direct."** Include just what's necessary; be concise; establish hierarchy. Check: does each element earn its place, does one or two things stand out, and is the most important thing first in reading order?
7. **Craft: "Care about every detail."** Quality sets the tone; experiment and iterate; maintain your craft. Check: are alignment, spacing, type, motion and wording deliberate, and does the screen hold up with real, worst-case content?
8. **Delight: "Make it human."** Identify the emotion you want to inspire; create defining moments; don't mistake delight for decoration; consider the whole. Check: what should the person feel here, and does nothing decorative stand in the way of the task?

## Nielsen's ten, as cross-check

1. **Visibility of system status.** Check: does the screen always show what is going on, with feedback within a reasonable time?
2. **Match between the system and the real world.** Check: does it speak the person's language, with no internal jargon, in a natural and logical order?
3. **User control and freedom.** Check: is there a clearly marked way out of every unwanted action, and undo?
4. **Consistency and standards.** Check: do the same words, situations and actions mean the same thing everywhere, following the platform's conventions?
5. **Error prevention.** Check: are error-prone conditions removed, or checked with a confirmation before the person commits?
6. **Recognition rather than recall.** Check: are the elements, actions and options the person needs visible or easy to retrieve, so nothing must be remembered?
7. **Flexibility and efficiency of use.** Check: can the practised person go faster, by shortcuts or by tailoring, without slowing the newcomer?
8. **Aesthetic and minimalist design.** Check: does the screen hold nothing irrelevant or rarely needed that competes with what matters?
9. **Help users recognize, diagnose, and recover from errors.** Check: does every error say in plain words what went wrong and how to fix it?
10. **Help and documentation.** Check: where help is needed, is it in context, searchable and in concrete steps?
