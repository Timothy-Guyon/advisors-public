# Method: how is the method itself held and changed?

The claims about the method as a whole: what it is, how a change to it is judged, and where its process comes from.

## A framework, not a doctrine

**The method is a framework adapted to each venture, not a blueprint of steps, and it is never allowed to harden into a pseudoscience of pivots and minimum viable products.** Ries warns that scientific management was set back by decades when its techniques were confused with its way of thinking, and that whoever adopts his method as a fixed list of tactics will fail. Its tools are there to build a practice that fits the venture. Check: is this rule being applied because it serves the learning here, or because it is the rule? Sources: Ries, chapter 12, Innovate (Becoming the status quo); chapter 13, Epilogue: Waste Not (Product development pseudoscience; In conclusion).

## A change to the method is judged by prediction

**A proposed change to the method is neither refused by dogma nor split down the middle; it is judged by whether its effect can be predicted, tried small and measured.** Splitting the difference rewards extreme positions, and refusing on principle shuts out improvement; theory is what lets a team predict a change's outcome and tell a problem caused by the new way of working from one with another cause. A switch to validated learning feels worse before it feels better, because the old way's costs are intangible and the new way's are plain. Check: what does this change to the method predict will happen, and how will it be seen whether it did? Sources: Ries, chapter 11, Adapt (opening); chapter 12, Innovate (Becoming the status quo); chapter 13, Epilogue: Waste Not (A new research program).

## Process grows out of problems

**Process is evolved continuously from the problems that actually occur, never built by stopping the work for a process project.** Ries's example is a training program that grew to excellence without anyone deciding to build one, each part added when root-cause analysis showed its lack was slowing the work. An adaptive organization adjusts its process to current conditions, a little at a time. Check: which problem that actually occurred does this addition to the process answer, and is it the smallest piece that answers it? Sources: Ries, chapter 11, Adapt (Building an adaptive organization; Make a proportional investment).
